Section 1

Executive Summary

Alert Dining is a mobile-ready platform that helps people with food allergies make safer, more informed choices when dining out. Users create a personal allergy profile from the "Top 14" allergens - either for themselves or on behalf of someone they care for (e.g., a child or dependent) - and the app displays participating restaurants and menus with personalized risk indicators (red / yellow / green) for each menu item.

Restaurants subscribe to be listed and manage their menu, ingredient, and allergen data. Diners access the platform for free.

StakeholderValue Delivered
DinerBrowse menus with personalized allergen risk signals at no cost
RestaurantAttract allergy-sensitive diners; showcase transparency and trust; unique QR code to drive app adoption
Event OrganizerOffer allergy-aware menus at conferences, catered events, and large gatherings via private, time-bound listings
Alert Dining TeamManage platform operations, restaurants, events, content, and billing

Section 4

How It Works

This section describes the primary user flows across the platform - from diner onboarding to restaurant management to special events to Alert Dining Team operations.

4.1 Diner Flow

1

Create Account & Allergy Profile

Diner signs up via email (Apple/Google optional). During onboarding, the diner selects whether they are creating a profile for themselves or on behalf of someone else (e.g., a parent or caregiver managing allergies for a child or dependent). This selection is captured for legal clarity and terms acceptance. The diner then selects allergens from the Top 14 list.

2

Discover Restaurants

App detects location (with permission) and uses Google Places API to surface nearby participating restaurants sorted by distance. Can also search by name.

3

Browse Menus

Diner opens a restaurant page and views menu items labeled Green, Yellow, or Red based on their personal allergen profile. Disclaimers remind the diner to confirm with staff.

4

View Item Details

Tapping an item shows a detailed view explaining why it was flagged - specific allergen match, ingredient-level detail (where provided by the restaurant), or cross-contact risk - along with any restaurant notes.

5

Scan a Restaurant or Event QR Code

Existing users scan a restaurant's QR code from within the Alert Dining app and are taken directly to that restaurant's profile. People who do not have the app use their phone's camera to scan the QR code and are directed to the App Store / Google Play to download Alert Dining. For special events, scanning the QR code and entering the event access code opens a private event menu.

6

Continue Using the App

Diner continues using the app for free. A ribbon-style ad placeholder is included in the architecture so ads and a premium ad-free tier can be introduced in a future phase.

7

Rate & Report (Phase 2)

Diner can rate an experience or report inaccurate menu/allergen information.

8

Generate Translation Card (Phase 3)

Diner generates an allergy translation card from their profile in up to 10 languages. Cards can be downloaded, shared, or saved directly to Apple Wallet / Google Wallet for instant offline access while traveling.

4.2 Restaurant Admin Flow

1

Sign Up & Request Verification

Restaurant admin creates an account and submits their listing for manual approval by the Alert Dining Team. Receives confirmation email.

2

Complete Restaurant Profile

Admin searches for their restaurant via Google Places API to auto-populate business name, address, hours, phone, and website. Reviews and confirms details, then selects a subscription tier and sets up billing via Stripe.

3

Complete Kitchen Questionnaire

Admin answers cross-contact questions: shared fryer use, dedicated prep areas, ingredient substitution frequency. This directly affects Yellow/Red indicator logic for all items.

4

Build & Tag Menu

Admin creates menu items (name, description, category) and selects one of two transparency paths: Option A - Full Ingredients: enter a complete ingredient list per item, from which allergen flags are derived. Option B - Allergen Tags Only: check allergen boxes from the Top 14 list per item without disclosing full ingredients. Optionally adds item-level notes (e.g., "Pesto contains pine nuts"). Full ingredient lists are used internally for risk calculation but are not displayed publicly to diners.

5

Publish & Maintain

Menu goes live after initial admin approval. Restaurant admin updates items and re-attests accuracy. "Last updated" timestamp displays to diners.

6

Generate Restaurant QR Code

After the restaurant profile is live, a unique, trackable QR code is generated. The restaurant can print and display the code on menus, signage, or table cards. Existing app users scan the code from within the Alert Dining app to go directly to the restaurant's profile. People without the app use their phone's camera to scan the code and are directed to the App Store / Google Play to download Alert Dining. QR scans are tracked for analytics.

7

View Analytics (Phase 2)

Admin views dashboard: menu views, unique diner counts, QR code scans, allergen demand breakdown, and trend charts.

4.3 Event Organizer Flow

The Special Events flow enables event organizers - such as conference planners, corporate catering teams, or large-venue hosts - to create private, time-bound allergy-aware menus for their attendees.

1

Create Event Account & Listing

Event organizer signs up and creates a new event listing with event name, date(s), description, and menu schedule (e.g., Monday lunch, Tuesday dinner). The listing is accessible via search, QR code, and event access code.

2

Build Event Menus & Tag Allergens

Organizer builds one or more menus (organized by day or meal) using the same menu item CRUD and allergen tagging (Option A or Option B) as restaurants. Green / Yellow / Red indicators work identically for attendees.

3

Generate QR Code & Event Access Code

A unique QR code and a 6-digit event access code are generated for the event. The organizer distributes these at registration, on signage, or via email to attendees. Scanning the QR code and entering the access code grants access to the private event menu.

4

Shared Login for Event Team

The event organizer uses a single shared login credential for their team to manage the event listing. Team members who need access receive the credential from the organizer.

5

Go Live & Deactivate

Organizer notifies Alert Dining when the event should go live and when it should be deactivated. After deactivation, the event listing is no longer accessible to attendees but is retained in the system for record-keeping.

4.4 Alert Dining Team (Management) Flow

The Alert Dining Team operates a dedicated admin portal to manage all platform activity. This is a distinct role with elevated access to approve restaurants and events, monitor content quality, manage billing, and maintain platform health.

Restaurant & Event Approval & Verification

1

Review Incoming Signup Requests

New restaurant and event organizer signups appear in a queue with submitted business/event details. Team reviews for completeness and legitimacy.

2

Approve or Reject Listing

Admin approves the restaurant (triggering the verified badge) or event, or rejects with a reason. Rejection sends an automated email. Approved restaurants become searchable by diners; approved events become accessible via QR code and access code.

3

Manual Verification Actions

Admin can manually revoke verification, flag a listing for review, or place a restaurant or event on hold if issues arise post-launch.

Menu & Content Moderation

4

Review Diner Reports

When diners report inaccurate allergen info or outdated menus, reports land in a moderation queue. Each report shows: the item flagged, the reason, and the diner who submitted it.

5

Investigate & Take Action

Admin reviews the report against the restaurant's tagged data. Actions available: mark resolved, send a warning to the restaurant, temporarily hide the item, or escalate for re-verification.

6

Edit or Override Menu Content

Admin can edit restaurant or event profile details or specific menu items if necessary to correct critical safety-related inaccuracies, with all edits logged in the audit trail.

7

Audit Log Review

Admin can view a chronological log of all changes to any menu item - who changed it, what changed, and when - to investigate disputes or spot patterns of inaccuracy.

Billing & Subscription Management

8

Monitor Subscription Status

Admin sees a dashboard of active restaurant and event organizer subscriptions: tier, billing status (active / past due / cancelled), and renewal dates.

9

Handle Billing Issues

Admin can manually upgrade or downgrade a restaurant's tier, issue credits, or mark a subscription as complimentary (e.g., during a pilot period).

10

Diner Subscription Oversight (Future)

When a diner premium tier is introduced in a future phase, admin will be able to view subscription counts, handle refund requests, and monitor churn trends.

Platform Health & Analytics

11

Dashboard Overview

Admin home screen surfaces: pending approvals count (restaurants + events), open moderation reports, active restaurant count, active event count, total MAU, and revenue MTD.

12

Allergen Demand Insights

Admin can view platform-wide allergen frequency data - which allergens appear most in diner profiles - to inform restaurant outreach and product decisions.

13

Allergen List Management

Admin manages the master list of recognized allergens. Changes cascade to all tagging interfaces.

14

User Management

Admin can search for any diner, restaurant, or event organizer account, view their profile/activity, reset passwords, or deactivate accounts if needed.

Section 7

Risk Indicator Logic

The red / yellow / green system uses three data inputs to generate each indicator:

  • User allergy profile (selected Top 14 allergens)
  • Restaurant kitchen questionnaire flags (cross-contact risk declarations)
  • Menu item allergen data - either derived from a full ingredient list (Option A) or from direct allergen tags (Option B), depending on the path chosen by the restaurant or event organizer

The Top 14 Allergens (Baseline)

The MVP launches with the following widely recognized allergens. Any additions will be confirmed during the Phase 0 requirements workshop.

  • Milk
  • Eggs
  • Peanuts
  • Tree nuts
  • Wheat
  • Soy
  • Fish
  • Shellfish (crustaceans)
  • Sesame
  • Mustard
  • Celery
  • Lupin
  • Mollusks
  • Sulfites
IndicatorTrigger Condition
REDItem directly contains a user allergen (via ingredient list or allergen tag), OR restaurant flagged high-risk cross-contact relevant to user allergens
YELLOWAmbiguous or possible cross-contact; restaurant indicated shared environment risk
GREENNo known allergen match based on current restaurant self-reported data (ingredient list or allergen tags)
⚠️ Safety language is always displayed alongside any indicator:
"Based on information provided by the restaurant at the time of your visit."
"Confirm with restaurant staff - ingredients and preparation may change without notice."

Section 13

Assumptions & Open Decisions

13.1 Timeline Transparency

Some aspects of this project may take longer than initially anticipated due to the complexity of third-party integrations, app store review processes, or unforeseen technical challenges. Should this happen, McG Works will be upfront with communication at all times - the client will be informed promptly of any delays, the reasons behind them, and revised timelines before they impact deliverables.

13.2 Assumptions

  • MVP starts with the Top 14 allergens
  • Restaurants choose between full ingredient input or allergen-only tagging per menu item; both paths supported from Phase 1
  • Full ingredient lists entered by restaurants are used internally for risk calculation and are not displayed publicly to diners unless allowed by the restaurant
  • Risk display uses red / yellow / green only (not percentage scores)
  • Phase 1 includes account type selection (self vs. on behalf of) for legal clarity; single-profile accounts only
  • Phase 3 translation cards include Apple Wallet and Google Wallet pass generation (requires Apple Developer account and Google Cloud project with Wallet API enabled - client-provided)
  • Special events use a shared login model for event teams (not individual team member accounts)

13.3 Open Decisions (to finalize in Phase 0)

  • Confirm Top 14 allergen list
  • Verification method: manual approval, domain email, or hybrid
  • Ad approach deferred to future phase; ribbon placeholder included in MVP architecture
  • Restaurant tier pricing (diner premium pricing deferred to future phase with ads)
  • Event organizer pricing model (subscription, per-event, or flat fee)
  • Whether basic reviews ship in Phase 1 or Phase 2
  • Confirm top 10 languages for Phase 3 translation cards

13.4 Future Capabilities (Post-Launch)

The following features are not in scope for the current engagement but have been discussed and will be architecturally accounted for where possible:

  • Family profiles: A single parent/caregiver account managing multiple allergy profiles (e.g., three children), with a profile switcher to toggle between profiles when browsing menus. MVP supports one profile per account; architecture will be designed to support multi-profile expansion.
  • In-app ordering: The platform may be expanded to support ordering directly from within the app at participating restaurants. This is a potential future direction and does not affect current scope or pricing.
  • Ads & premium ad-free tier: The MVP includes a ribbon-style ad placeholder in the diner app. In a future phase, ads can be activated - with restaurants promoting locally by zip code or radius, national brands (food brands, allergy-friendly products, travel brands, etc.) advertising across the platform, and all ads submitted through a dashboard and approved by the Alert Dining team before going live. A premium subscription tier will also be introduced, allowing diners to upgrade for an ad-free experience.
