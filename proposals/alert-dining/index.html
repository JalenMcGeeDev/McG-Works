Section 1

Executive Summary

Alert Dining is a mobile-ready platform that helps people with food allergies make safer, more informed choices when dining out. Users create a personal allergy profile from the "Top 14" allergens, and the app displays participating restaurants and menus with personalized risk indicators (red / yellow / green) for each menu item.

Restaurants subscribe to be listed and manage their menu and allergen data. Diners access the platform for free (with ads) or upgrade to a premium tier (no ads). A later phase adds allergy translation cards that can be downloaded and saved offline for travel use.

StakeholderValue Delivered
Diner (Free)Browse menus with personalized allergen risk signals at no cost
Diner (Premium)Same experience, ad-free
RestaurantAttract allergy-sensitive diners; showcase transparency and trust
Alert Dining TeamManage platform operations, restaurants, content, and billing

Section 4

How It Works

This section describes the primary user flows across the platform — from diner onboarding to restaurant management to Alert Dining Team operations.

4.1 Diner Flow

1

Create Account & Allergy Profile

Diner signs up via email (Apple/Google optional). Selects allergens from the Top 14 list.

2

Discover Restaurants

App detects location (with permission). Diner sees a list of participating restaurants sorted by distance. Can also search by name.

3

Browse Menus

Diner opens a restaurant page and views menu items labeled Green, Yellow, or Red based on their personal allergen profile. Disclaimers remind the diner to confirm with staff.

4

View Item Details

Tapping an item shows a detailed view explaining why it was flagged — specific allergen match or cross-contact risk — along with any restaurant notes.

5

Upgrade or Continue Free

Diner continues for free (with ads) or upgrades to premium to remove ads. Subscription managed in-app.

6

Rate & Report (Phase 2)

Diner can rate an experience or report inaccurate menu/allergen information.

7

Generate Translation Card (Phase 3)

Diner generates a downloadable allergy translation card from their profile in up to 2 languages for offline travel use.

4.2 Restaurant Admin Flow

1

Sign Up & Request Verification

Restaurant admin creates an account and submits their listing for manual approval by the Alert Dining Team. Receives confirmation email.

2

Complete Restaurant Profile

Admin enters business name, address, hours, phone, and website. Selects a subscription tier and sets up billing via Stripe.

3

Complete Kitchen Questionnaire

Admin answers cross-contact questions: shared fryer use, dedicated prep areas, ingredient substitution frequency. This directly affects Yellow/Red indicator logic for all items.

4

Build & Tag Menu

Admin creates menu items (name, description, category) and checks allergen boxes from the Top 14 list per item. Optionally adds item-level notes (e.g., "Pesto contains pine nuts").

5

Publish & Maintain

Menu goes live after initial admin approval. Restaurant admin updates items and re-attests accuracy. "Last updated" timestamp displays to diners.

6

View Analytics (Phase 2)

Admin views dashboard: menu views, unique diner counts, allergen demand breakdown, and trend charts.

4.3 Alert Dining Team (Management) Flow

The Alert Dining Team operates a dedicated admin portal to manage all platform activity. This is a distinct role with elevated access to approve restaurants, monitor content quality, manage billing, and maintain platform health.

Restaurant Approval & Verification

1

Review Incoming Signup Requests

New restaurant signups appear in a queue with submitted business details. Team reviews for completeness and legitimacy.

2

Approve or Reject Listing

Admin approves the restaurant (triggering the verified badge) or rejects with a reason. Rejection sends an automated email to the restaurant. Approved restaurants become searchable by diners.

3

Manual Verification Actions

Admin can manually revoke verification, flag a listing for review, or place a restaurant on hold if issues arise post-launch.

Menu & Content Moderation

4

Review Diner Reports

When diners report inaccurate allergen info or outdated menus, reports land in a moderation queue. Each report shows: the item flagged, the reason, and the diner who submitted it.

5

Investigate & Take Action

Admin reviews the report against the restaurant's tagged data. Actions available: mark resolved, send a warning to the restaurant, temporarily hide the item, or escalate for re-verification.

6

Edit or Override Menu Content

Admin can edit restaurant profile details or specific menu items if necessary to correct critical safety-related inaccuracies, with all edits logged in the audit trail.

7

Audit Log Review

Admin can view a chronological log of all changes to any menu item — who changed it, what changed, and when — to investigate disputes or spot patterns of inaccuracy.

Billing & Subscription Management

8

Monitor Subscription Status

Admin sees a dashboard of active restaurant subscriptions: tier, billing status (active / past due / cancelled), and renewal dates.

9

Handle Billing Issues

Admin can manually upgrade or downgrade a restaurant's tier, issue credits, or mark a subscription as complimentary (e.g., during a pilot period).

10

Diner Premium Oversight

Admin can view premium diner subscription counts, handle refund requests, and monitor churn trends.

Platform Health & Analytics

11

Dashboard Overview

Admin home screen surfaces: pending approvals count, open moderation reports, active restaurant count, total MAU, and revenue MTD.

12

Allergen Demand Insights

Admin can view platform-wide allergen frequency data — which allergens appear most in diner profiles — to inform restaurant outreach and product decisions.

13

Allergen List Management

Admin manages the master list of recognized allergens (Top 14 definitions, regional variations, additions over time). Changes cascade to all tagging interfaces.

14

User Management

Admin can search for any diner or restaurant account, view their profile/activity, reset passwords, or deactivate accounts if needed.

Section 7

Risk Indicator Logic

The red / yellow / green system uses three data inputs to generate each indicator:

  • User allergy profile (selected Top 14 allergens)
  • Restaurant kitchen questionnaire flags (cross-contact risk declarations)
  • Menu item allergen tags (restaurant-entered per item)
IndicatorTrigger Condition
REDItem directly contains a user allergen, OR restaurant flagged high-risk cross-contact relevant to user allergens
YELLOWAmbiguous or possible cross-contact; restaurant indicated shared environment risk
GREENNo known allergen match based on current restaurant self-reported data
⚠️ Safety language is always displayed alongside any indicator:
"Based on information provided by the restaurant at the time of your visit."
"Confirm with restaurant staff — ingredients and preparation may change without notice."

Section 9

Legal & Liability Safeguards

  • Clear disclaimers presented during diner onboarding and on every item detail view
  • Restaurant attestation checkbox required every time menu changes are published
  • "Verified" badge confirms identity verification only — not a medical safety certification
  • "Report inaccurate info" flow available to all diners (Phase 1 basic, Phase 2 enhanced)
  • Admin audit log provides traceability for all content changes

Section 13

Assumptions & Open Decisions

13.1 Assumptions

  • MVP starts with the Top 14 allergens (regional definitions confirmed in Phase 0)
  • Restaurants tag allergens by category, not full ingredient lists
  • Risk display uses red / yellow / green only (not percentage scores)
  • Phase 3 translation cards are downloadable files, not Apple/Google Wallet passes

13.2 Open Decisions (to finalize in Phase 0)

  • Confirm Top 14 allergen list and regional definitions
  • Verification method: manual approval, domain email, or hybrid
  • Ad approach: simple in-house banners or lightweight ad network
  • Restaurant tier pricing and diner premium pricing
  • Whether basic reviews ship in Phase 1 or Phase 2
  • Additional language for Phase 3 translation cards
